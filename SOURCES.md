# Sources, conventions, and editorial policy

Line Atlas is a synthesis, not a redistribution of a textbook or database. All
explanatory prose and diagrams are original. Numerical line data are paired
with source keys so that readers can return to the authoritative record.

## Source hierarchy

1. Critically evaluated data: NIST Atomic Spectra Database for atomic and ionic
   wavelengths and levels; CDMS, JPL, and Splatalogue for molecular and radio
   transitions; CHIANTI for collisionally ionized plasma diagnostics.
2. Standard graduate texts: Draine (2011); Osterbrock & Ferland (2006); Dopita
   & Sutherland (2003); Rybicki & Lightman (1979); Tielens (2005).
3. Peer-reviewed review articles and primary papers for environments,
   diagnostics, calibration limits, and survey results.
4. Official observatory documentation for wavelength coverage and observing
   modes. Instrument entries are explicitly approximate and must be checked
   against the current call-for-proposals documentation.

## Wavelength convention

- Values below 2000 Angstrom are normally vacuum wavelengths.
- Optical values may historically be quoted in standard air; the catalogue
  records the convention rather than silently mixing systems.
- Molecular rotational transitions are represented by rest frequency when that
  is the native and least ambiguous coordinate.
- Rounded blends, multiplets, and conventional labels (for example, [O II]
  3727) are distinguished from component wavelengths.

## Physical-regime labels

Temperature and density fields are deliberately described as regimes. They do
not imply that a transition forms at a unique temperature or density. The line
strength may depend on ionization parameter, abundance pattern, radiation-field
shape, optical depth, non-equilibrium history, colliders, and geometry.

## Corrections

Corrections should identify the catalogue record, proposed value, convention,
and an authoritative source. Precision updates should not add significant
figures unsupported by the source.
